Role: Independent causal study, RStudio.

Used a difference-in-differences design with state and year fixed effects (2006–2019) to isolate the effect of Colorado's 2012 legalization on unemployment.

Outcome: Found a statistically significant 0.70-point reduction versus control states, confirmed with an event-study robustness check — a policy-relevant causal result, not just correlation.

Hispanic Earnings Gap in STEM

Role: Independent econometric study, Stata.

Estimated OLS models with interaction terms on 77,796 observations to test whether returns to education in STEM differ by ethnicity.

Outcome: Found a 9.7% baseline earnings gap that narrows to ~3% at the Master's level and disappears at the PhD/professional level — while advanced degrees raised earnings 14–79% overall.

Commercial Awareness

Where the analytics market is headed

What I'm watching as I move from a single industry into cross-industry analytics roles.

01 / MARKET

Demand is up, but selectivity is too

Analyst hiring is growing well ahead of overall job creation this year, even as companies stay cautious about headcount broadly. That combination rewards candidates who can show finished, measurable work rather than coursework alone — which is exactly why this portfolio leads with outcomes, not just tools.

02 / SKILL SHIFT

AI is raising the floor, not replacing the analyst

Most analysts now use AI tools day-to-day for cleaning and first-pass analysis, which is shifting the job toward judgment: framing the right question, validating the model, and explaining the "so what" to a non-technical stakeholder. That's the same translation work I did running Minitab training for plant staff.

03 / WHERE THE JOBS ARE

Demand is no longer industry-locked

Manufacturing, finance, healthcare, and retail are all investing in analytics teams for the same reason: decisions are getting more data-dependent everywhere, not just in tech. That's the case for my hybrid track — the modeling and automation skills I built on a factory floor transfer directly.

MY TAKE

The gap I keep seeing in the market isn't technical — plenty of people can run a regression or build a dashboard. It's the analyst who can sit with a plant manager or a business lead, understand what they actually need to decide, and hand back something they'll trust enough to act on. That's the muscle I built at Mohawk, and it's the one I'm applying everywhere I take this next.
